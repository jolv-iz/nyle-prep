2. What “done” looks like per subject

Not full mastery — for an open-book, 50-question, 2-hour exam, “done” means: 1. A one-page index in the same trigger→answer format as the CPLR docs, covering the recurring NY-specific traps for that subject. 2. 3–5 scenario drills survived without the index, to confirm the pattern is internalized (index is a backup, not a crutch). 3. Index is fast to navigate — you could find the right section in ~20 seconds under exam pressure.

3. Proposed schedule (39 days) — INTERLEAVED, not single-subject blocks

Per user preference: no single-subject-per-day drilling. Each session mixes multiple subjects, scenario by scenario, unannounced — closer to real exam conditions (you don’t know what’s coming next) and better for retention than block practice.

Structure: each session rotates through a pool of “active” subjects (roughly 3–4 at a time), pulling scenario order at random rather than grouped. A subject stays in the active pool until it hits the “done” bar (Section 2), then rotates out and a new one rotates in. Indexes still get built per-subject (that artifact doesn’t change) — only the drilling order is mixed.

Daily cycle: 1. 50 Q cold drill, mixed/unannounced order, no notes. 2. Immediate scoring, tagged by subject + topic branch. 3. Same-session re-drill on misses — new scenario variants, application-first. 4. Log to running error tracker (below).

Review cadence: every 4th day = comprehensive review, not fresh material — retests everything flagged wrong across the prior 3 days. Rationale: spaced-repetition retention is highest when review lands 2–5 days after first exposure; every 4 days balances that against needing to keep covering new ground in 39 days. ~7 review days total across the drilling phase.
